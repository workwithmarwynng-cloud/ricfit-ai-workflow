# Career Positioning

## Purpose

Career positioning is the stage where the RicFit AI Remote Job Workflow converts the player's existing background into a realistic professional direction for remote employment.

The objective is to identify where the player's real experience and transferable capabilities can provide the strongest professional positioning.

The system does not create experience that does not exist.

## Positioning Principle

The central principle is:

**Make the player's real experience as relevant as possible without misrepresenting it.**

The workflow can improve how existing experience is presented, but it cannot create unsupported qualifications.

## Positioning Process

```text
Player Background
        ↓
Existing Experience
        ↓
Skills & Capabilities
        ↓
Transferable Skills
        ↓
Target Role Requirements
        ↓
Career Direction
        ↓
Professional Positioning



Starting With the Player

The system begins with the player's actual background rather than beginning with a predetermined job title.

Relevant evidence may include:

Employment history
Education
Certifications
Technical skills
Customer-facing experience
Administrative experience
Leadership
Communication
Sports experience
Technology exposure
Other documented responsibilities

The purpose is to determine what the player can credibly bring to an employer.

Identifying Career Direction

The workflow considers realistic remote career directions based on the player's background.

Potential directions include:

Customer Support
Customer Service
Customer Success
Client Support
Operations Support
Administrative Support
Virtual Assistant
Sales Support
Appointment Setting
DM Setting
Lead Generation
Sales Development

These categories are examples of potential directions rather than fixed assignments.

The appropriate direction depends on the player's actual experience, capabilities, preferences, and objectives.

Transferable Skills

Transferable skills allow the system to connect existing experience with the requirements of a target role.

Relevant transferable capabilities can include:

Leadership
Communication
Teamwork
Discipline
Accountability
Time management
Decision making
Adaptability
Working under pressure
Consistency
Coachability
Responsibility

The workflow determines which of these capabilities are relevant to the target role.

Sports Experience as Professional Evidence

Sports experience can provide evidence of workplace-relevant capabilities.

However, the workflow does not present athletic participation as unrelated professional employment.

Instead, it translates legitimate capabilities developed through sports into professional language when those capabilities are relevant.

For example:

Sports Experience
        ↓
Demonstrated Capability
        ↓
Workplace-Relevant Skill
        ↓
Target Role Relevance

This allows sports experience to strengthen professional positioning without misrepresenting the player's employment history.

Experience-to-Role Mapping

Career positioning uses a mapping process:

Existing Experience
        ↓
What Did the Player Actually Do?
        ↓
What Skills Does That Demonstrate?
        ↓
Which Target Roles Value Those Skills?
        ↓
How Should the Experience Be Presented?

The emphasis is on evidence rather than assumptions.

Professional Narrative

Once the strongest direction has been identified, the workflow establishes a professional narrative.

The narrative should communicate:

What the player can do
What experience supports those capabilities
Which roles are appropriate
Which transferable skills are relevant
Why the player can credibly pursue the target direction

The narrative should remain grounded in the player's actual background.

Target Role Alignment

Positioning becomes stronger when the player's experience is connected directly to target-role requirements.

The workflow therefore considers:

Target Role Requirement
        ↓
Relevant Player Experience
        ↓
Supporting Skill
        ↓
Professional Evidence

This helps prevent generic positioning.

Avoiding Over-Positioning

The system should avoid positioning the player as something they cannot credibly support.

For example, the workflow should not:

Create senior-level experience
Invent management responsibilities
Claim technical expertise without evidence
Add certifications that were not provided
Claim software proficiency without support
Convert sports participation into fictional employment

Strong positioning does not require exaggeration.

Income and Career Direction

When an income target is provided, it can influence career targeting.

The workflow may use the target to help prioritize appropriate role categories and compensation levels.

However, an income target is treated as an objective rather than a guarantee.

The system should not imply that a particular job or income level is assured.

Positioning for Remote Work

The system emphasizes capabilities that are relevant to remote work when supported by the player's background.

These may include:

Communication
Reliability
Organization
Time management
Self-management
Customer interaction
Digital tools
Documentation
Team collaboration
Accountability

The objective is to make the player's existing capabilities understandable to remote employers.

Relationship to the Resume

Career positioning occurs before document generation.

The resume should reflect the positioning strategy rather than independently inventing a new narrative.

Career Analysis

Human Judgment

AI can assist with identifying patterns, transferable skills, and potential career directions.

Human judgment remains important when deciding:

Whether a career direction is realistic
Whether a transferable skill is sufficiently supported
Whether positioning could be misleading
Which experience deserves emphasis
Whether additional information is required

The operator remains accountable for the final positioning decision.

Anti-Fabrication Boundary

Career positioning must remain inside the boundary of verified information.

The workflow may:

Reframe existing experience
Improve professional wording
Highlight relevant responsibilities
Identify transferable skills
Connect existing capabilities to target roles

The workflow may not:

Invent employment
Invent responsibilities
Invent achievements
Invent certifications
Invent metrics
Invent software experience
Claim unsupported qualifications
Design Objective

The objective of career positioning is to create the strongest credible professional direction available from the player's actual background.

The desired outcome is:

Relevant without exaggeration.

Professional without fabrication.

Targeted without becoming misleading.

This positioning then becomes the foundation for the remainder of the remote job application workflow.
