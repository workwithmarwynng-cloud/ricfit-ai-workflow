# Input Analysis

## Purpose

Input analysis is the first decision-making stage of the RicFit AI Remote Job Workflow.

Before generating application documents, the system establishes a structured understanding of the player's background, career direction, transferable skills, and remote employment objectives.

The purpose is to ensure that later outputs are based on the player's actual information rather than assumptions.

## Core Inputs

The workflow can use structured information such as:

- Player name
- Location
- Email
- Phone number
- Education
- Certifications
- Employment history
- Job titles
- Employment dates
- Professional responsibilities
- Skills
- Software and tools
- Sports background
- Career interests
- Remote work preferences
- Income target
- Existing professional profiles
- Other relevant background information

These inputs establish the factual foundation for the application package.

## Input Principle

The workflow treats candidate information as the source of truth for professional claims.

The system should not create experience simply because it would make a candidate appear more competitive.

Instead, the workflow identifies the strongest legitimate interpretation of the information that has been provided.

## Background Analysis

The first analytical step is understanding the player's complete background.

The workflow considers both traditional professional experience and other evidence that may demonstrate workplace-relevant capabilities.

Relevant areas can include:

- Previous employment
- Education
- Certifications
- Technical skills
- Customer-facing experience
- Administrative responsibilities
- Leadership experience
- Team environments
- Communication experience
- Sports experience
- Technology exposure
- Operational responsibilities

The objective is to determine what the player can credibly present to an employer.

## Career Direction Analysis

The system then considers which remote career directions are most appropriate.

Potential directions include:

- Customer Support
- Customer Service
- Customer Success
- Client Support
- Operations Support
- Administrative Support
- Virtual Assistant
- Sales Support
- Appointment Setting
- DM Setting
- Lead Generation
- Sales Development

The system does not assume that every player should pursue the same category.

Career direction is based on the player's actual background, strengths, transferable skills, and stated objectives.

## Transferable Skills Analysis

A key component of the input analysis is identifying legitimate transferable skills.

Sports experience can provide evidence of professional qualities including:

- Leadership
- Communication
- Teamwork
- Discipline
- Accountability
- Time management
- Decision making
- Adaptability
- Working under pressure
- Consistency
- Coachability
- Responsibility

These qualities can support professional positioning when they are relevant to the target role.

The workflow does not treat participation in sports as equivalent to unrelated professional employment.

Instead, sports experience is translated into legitimate workplace-relevant evidence.

## Experience Mapping

The system maps existing experience against potential target roles.

The basic logic is:

```text
Existing Experience
        ↓
Relevant Skills
        ↓
Transferable Capabilities
        ↓
Target Role Requirements
        ↓
Professional Positioning



This allows the workflow to identify where the player's existing experience has the strongest relevance.

Job Targeting Inputs

Job targeting can incorporate:

Career direction
Existing experience
Transferable skills
Technical capabilities
Communication strengths
Remote suitability
Income requirements
Professional interests

The objective is to establish realistic job categories before producing application materials.

Income Target

When an income target is provided, it becomes part of the job-search strategy.

The target can be used to help determine:

Appropriate role categories
Compensation expectations
Application priorities
Full-time versus part-time considerations
Hourly versus monthly compensation comparisons

Income targets are treated as planning requirements rather than employment guarantees.

Information Gaps

The workflow identifies information that may be incomplete or require confirmation.

Examples include:

Missing employment dates
Unclear job titles
Missing responsibilities
Unverified certifications
Unclear software experience
Missing contact information
Ambiguous career objectives
Missing income requirements

Information gaps should be resolved or clearly handled before important claims are finalized.

Anti-Fabrication Analysis

The input-analysis stage establishes the factual boundary for the entire workflow.

The system must not invent:

Employers
Job responsibilities
Certifications
Achievements
Metrics
Software experience
Employment history
Professional experience

If a desired qualification is not supported by the player's information, the workflow should not manufacture it.

Professional Positioning Logic

The central positioning principle is:

Make the player's real experience as relevant as possible without misrepresenting it.

This means the system can:

Reframe relevant experience
Highlight transferable skills
Improve professional language
Emphasize relevant responsibilities
Connect existing skills to target roles

But it cannot:

Create employment history
Invent achievements
Claim unsupported qualifications
Present sports participation as unrelated professional employment

        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
Transferable Skills
        ↓
Career Direction
        ↓
Target Job Categories
        ↓

This working profile becomes the foundation for the document-production stages.

Relationship to Document Generation

Input analysis occurs before the standardized document sequence.

The workflow should not immediately generate the resume simply because information has been provided.

Instead:

INPUT
Player Information
        ↓
ANALYSIS
Background + Skills
        ↓
STRATEGY
Career Direction + Job Targeting
        ↓
POSITIONING
Professional Narrative
        ↓
PRODUCTION
Application Package

This separation between analysis and production is a core design feature of the workflow.

Human Review

AI can assist with organizing and interpreting the information, but the operator remains responsible for confirming important facts and positioning decisions.

Human review is particularly important when:

Information is ambiguous
Experience is difficult to classify
Transferable skills require interpretation
Career direction is uncertain
A claim could materially affect the application
Additional information is required

The system therefore uses AI for leverage while maintaining human accountability.

Design Objective

The objective of input analysis is to create a reliable foundation for the rest of the workflow.

A strong analysis stage allows the system to produce documents that are:

Factually grounded
Role relevant
Consistent
Professionally positioned
Transferable
Practical for remote job searching
